Negative for deployment, and it fails upward. Guided filtering and every other spatial post-process tried here deleted change, because a marginal class loses a neighbourhood vote. Phoenix does not vote — it draws. Handed a 6-pixel blob it returns the object it thinks is there, and at 10 m there is no object.

The change class is 992 components with a median of 6 px. The 650 under 10 px are grown 2.3× and supply 57% of every added pixel; above 30 px the method holds its area and moves the boundary instead, reaching IoU 0.46–0.66. If a refiner is ever wanted here it has to be size-gated to those.

Capacity does not help, it hurts: swapping EfficientViT for the paper's ViT-H grows those same sub-10 px blobs 8× instead of 2.3×. A stronger encoder is more confident about the object it is hallucinating, which is the clearest evidence that the problem is the scale of the thing being refined rather than the refiner.

The alignment gain is real and controlled: 1.425 → 1.494, against 1.448 for growing the input uniformly to the identical area and 1.004 for the same blobs relocated at random. Roughly two thirds of it is content. It costs agreement with its own input of 0.366 — against a map that reproduces itself across seed blocks at 0.842.

Rasters, delta layers and the stretched backdrop are written for QGIS under data/inference/phoenix_oslo_explore/. Method and controls: section U5 of docs/research/S2_DETAIL_RESEARCH.md. Phoenix is deterministic here — two independent runs agreed on every pixel.
